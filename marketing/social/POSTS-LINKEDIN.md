## 1 · Launch (post this before you invite anyone)

*Image: `post-square-1200x1200.png`*

> A renter in Brooklyn pays a broker fee. The renter next door, same building,
> same week, pays nothing.
>
> Since the FARE Act took effect in June 2025, the rule is simple: whoever hires
> the broker pays the broker. If a landlord's agent lists the apartment, that
> fee is the landlord's. The one case where a renter pays is when the renter
> hires their own agent — and agrees the fee in writing first.
>
> That second case had no good front door. So we built one.
>
> RentLeaks Broker Network, live today:
>
> • A renter says what they're looking for and the most they'll pay a broker.
> • Up to three verified, licensed agents who work that area send a short pitch and a fee at or under that cap. Proposals above it can't be sent.
> • The renter picks one — or none — and signs one plain-English representation and fee agreement in the app, with a time-stamped record and a copy for everyone.
> • No lease, no fee. Nothing is owed to RentLeaks.
>
> For licensed agents: leads arrive with the brief, the budget and the fee cap
> already on them. No sign-up fee, no charge per lead. A broker-to-broker
> referral fee is invoiced to your brokerage only after a lease is signed —
> the way RPL § 442 requires.
>
> Renters start here: rentleaks.com/hire-a-broker
> Agents apply here: rentleaks.com/hire-a-broker/agents.html
>
> #rentals #realestate #NYCrealestate #FAREAct #proptech

## 2 · The explainer that earns saves

*Image: `post-renters-1200x627.png`* · link: `?utm_source=linkedin&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=fare-act`

> Four questions I get every week about broker fees, answered in the order
> people ask them.
>
> **"The listing says no fee — is that a deal?"** It's the default now for any
> home a landlord's agent is listing. Not a favour, not a negotiation.
>
> **"Can an agent charge me to show me their own landlord's apartment?"** No.
> And they have to tell you in writing before the viewing if they represent that
> landlord.
>
> **"So why would I ever pay one?"** Because you hired them — to search, book
> viewings, know the buildings, and negotiate for you. That's a service you're
> choosing, at a price you agreed first.
>
> **"What should that cost?"** Quotes come as months of rent, a percentage of
> the year, or a flat fee. One month is roughly 8.33% of a year, so convert
> before you compare: on a $3,600 apartment, "one month" is $3,600 and "15%" is
> $6,480. Same apartment, $2,880 apart.
>
> We put the rest in a five-page playbook — what a fair agreement says, a fee-cap
> worksheet, and twelve questions to ask before you hire anyone. Free, no strings:
> rentleaks.com/hire-a-broker/guide.html

## 4 · Show the machinery (trust post)

*Image: `post-square-1200x1200.png`*

> We ask renters to sign something. So here's exactly what it says, in public.
>
> The tenant representation and fee agreement, ten sections:
>
> 1. Who's who — your agent's name, licence number, brokerage, supervising broker.
> 2. What they'll do — area, home type, budget, dates, and that they work for you.
> 3. How long — 90 days, or until you sign a lease. Either side can end it by email.
> 4. The fee — the exact number you accepted, earned only if you lease a home they found or showed you.
> 5. Their promises — written notice if they also represent a home's landlord, no fee for those homes, no apartment held hostage to hiring them, fair housing throughout.
> 6. Your money — rent and deposits go to the landlord against a signed lease. Never before a viewing.
> 7. The referral — their brokerage pays us out of its fee. It never increases what you pay.
> 8. Your details — used for your search, not sold.
> 9. Signing — consent first, time-stamped, a copy for everyone, and a SHA-256 fingerprint so nobody can change a word afterwards.
> 10. Law — the state you're renting in.
>
> If an agreement you're handed anywhere doesn't cover those, ask why.
>
> rentleaks.com/hire-a-broker
>

## Comment replies worth having ready

- **"Isn't this just another lead seller?"** → "No fee to join and no charge per lead — we're paid a referral percentage by the brokerage after a lease, so if you don't earn, we don't."
- **"How do you verify licences?"** → "State licence lookup before the first lead, with the name, status and supervising broker checked against the application. We countersign only after that."
- **"Do you take a cut from the renter?"** → "Nothing. The renter's fee goes to their broker's brokerage, and our referral percentage comes out of that fee — it never adds to it."
- **"What about outside New York?"** → "The agreements are built for NY first; we add a state when we have a broker licence and partners there. Tell us where you work and we'll tell you honestly if we're live."
