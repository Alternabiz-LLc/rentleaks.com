```
The rule most New York renters still haven't been told.

Since the FARE Act took effect on 11 June 2025, the person who hires the broker pays the broker. If a landlord hired an agent to list and show an apartment, that agent's fee is the landlord's cost — it can't be passed to you, and every fee you do pay has to be itemised in writing before you sign.
```

```
New York renters pay fees that the law stopped allowing in 2019.

The security deposit is capped at one month's rent. Application and move-in charges are barred, and a background or credit check can cost you no more than $20 — which you don't have to pay at all if you hand over a consumer report you already have, dated within the last 30 days.

If a landlord or agent asks for more, the correct response is to ask which statute allows it.
```

## Day 12 · nyc-sublet

**Image:** `images/social/ig/12-nyc-sublet.jpg`  ·  **Link:** https://rentleaks.com/lease-break.html

```
Breaking a New York lease is less hopeless than it feels.

In a building with four or more units, you send a proper written sublet request. The landlord has 10 days to ask for more information and 30 days from that to decide — and if they don't answer, consent is deemed given. Assignment is the harder route: it needs written consent, which can be refused without a reason, but then your remedy is release from the lease on 30 days' notice.

Lease-break posts are free on RentLeaks. Link in bio.

General information, not legal advice.

Source: NY Real Property Law § 226-b — sublet and assignment
```

```
Amsterdam is one of the strongest positions a mid-term renter can be in, and most people don't know it.

A stay of 30 days or more is a tenancy with full protection. The deposit is capped at two months. And under the good landlordship act, a letting agent working for the landlord cannot charge the tenant a fee — the same principle New York adopted five years later.
```

## Day 15 · berlin

**Image:** `images/social/ig/15-berlin.jpg`  ·  **Link:** https://rentleaks.com/cities.html

```
Berlin is the market where the usual 30-day rule doesn't apply.

The Zweckentfremdungsverbot — the ban on misappropriating housing — is what actually binds, and in practice the threshold sits nearer three months than thirty days. A shorter let needs registration, and the contract has to be a Zeitmietvertrag with a documented temporary purpose.

This is why "it works in Amsterdam" is not a plan for Berlin. We keep the rules per market so a listing is written to the right regime from the start.

General information, not legal advice.

Source: Berlin — Zweckentfremdungsverbot
```
